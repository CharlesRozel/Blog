Charles Rozel is the pen name of an English lawyer and author known for writing comedy thrillers. He also specialises in Regency and Victorian detectives.
Rozel’s comedy thrillers, Dead Guy and Dead Ghost, are unique for blending murder with lighthearted humour and romantic subplots, set against quirky British backdrops. 
Dead Guy exemplifies this style by opening with a seemingly supernatural voice from a coffin declaring, “I was murdered,” which kicks off a murder investigation led by Inspector Robert Peel of the Quaystone Cops, the main protagonist in the series.  There is also horror such as a dancing scarecrow and a real body on a Guy Fawkes bonfire.  
Peel is aided in his cases by Sarah Wilde, a glamorous lawyer from London. They have a love-hate relationship, complicated by the arrival on the scene of Captain Rupert Sutherland, a dashing Special Forces officer. Sutherland and his platoon - the argumentative Sergeant Dibbs, the combative Sapper Napper and Corporal McDuff, the Glaswegian communications specialist - are there to help in the hunt for a serial killer. Peel and Sutherland become rivals for Sarah's affection. 
The third novel featuring Peel and Sarah Wilde will be published in 2027.
 “Murder is not funny but these books are.”
